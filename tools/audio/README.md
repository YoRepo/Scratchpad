# Audio: synthesised music and effects

Every sound the game plays is generated by `tools/audio/synth.py`. There are no samples and no
downloads: numpy renders the music and the effects from scratch, ffmpeg encodes the music to Ogg
Vorbis, and the effects stay 16-bit WAV. The generator is deterministic (fixed seed, pinned
encoder flags), so running it again produces byte-identical files; commit what it writes.

```sh
python3 tools/audio/synth.py                        # rewrites game/assets/audio/**
python3 tools/audio/synth.py --scratch /some/dir    # also keeps the WAV masters and a 10 s
                                                    # preview Ogg of each theme in /some/dir
python3 tools/audio/synth.py --only coin town_theme # just these
```

Needs `python3` with `numpy` and `scipy`, and `ffmpeg` built with `libvorbis`. A full run takes
about 30 s and ends with a quality table; it exits non-zero when any check fails. After
regenerating, run Godot's `--import` so the `*.import` sidecars and the cache are refreshed, and
commit the sidecars with the files.

## What is where

```
game/assets/audio/music/town_theme.ogg        calm loop for the town map
game/assets/audio/music/outskirts_theme.ogg   brighter loop for the maps outside the walls
game/assets/audio/sfx/<name>.wav              one-shot effects, see the catalogue below
game/src/core/sound_bank.gd                   autoload SoundBank: play_sfx / play_music / stop_music
tools/audio/synth.py                          the generator (instruments, scores, effects, checks)
```

Playback goes through the `SoundBank` autoload, which loads lazily by name so adding a sound is:
generate it, then call it.

```gdscript
SoundBank.play_sfx(&"footstep_1", -6.0, true)   # name, volume offset in dB, randomise pitch ±4 %
SoundBank.play_music(&"town_theme")             # cross-fades from whatever is playing (1 s default)
SoundBank.play_music(&"outskirts_theme", 2.5)   # longer cross-fade
SoundBank.stop_music()                          # fade out and stop
```

A missing file warns once (`push_warning`) and is otherwise ignored, so gameplay code never has
to check whether a sound exists. Music streams are set to loop in code; the Ogg files are made to
loop seamlessly (below), so do not fade them at the loop point. `SoundBank.music_volume_db`
(-6 dB) trims all music under the effects. Everything plays on the Master bus.

## Effects catalogue

All effects are mono 44.1 kHz 16-bit WAV, peak-normalised to -3 dBFS, 0.05 to 1.2 s long.

| File | Intended use | Length | Made of |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `jump` | knight leaves the ground | 0.21 s | rising harmonic sweep (300 to 790 Hz) and a puff of band-passed noise |
| `land` | knight lands | 0.20 s | pitch-dropping sine thud, low-passed noise "dust" |
| `footstep_1`, `footstep_2` | alternate on each step | 0.10 s | two short band-passed noise bursts (heel, toe) plus a low scuff; different bands per variant |
| `sword_swing` | attack starts | 0.30 s | three noise bands whose envelopes peak in turn (low, mid, high), a faint steel ring |
| `hit_enemy` | sword connects with a soft target | 0.24 s | thud, crunch and snap noise bands, lightly soft-clipped |
| `hit_shell` | sword connects with the snail's shell | 0.15 s | hard click plus five short-ringing partials (640 Hz to 5.2 kHz) |
| `hurt_player` | knight takes damage | 0.38 s | down-gliding odd-harmonic tone (440 to 330 Hz), soft-clipped, with a thud |
| `enemy_die` | a mob dies | 0.65 s | wobbling two-octave downward sweep and a late low-passed "poof" |
| `coin` | money pickup | 0.42 s | two bell pings, B5 then E6 (inharmonic partial stacks) |
| `item_pickup` | any other pickup | 0.55 s | harp arpeggio D5 A5 D6 with a touch of reverb |
| `quest_accept` | quest taken | 0.85 s | lute arpeggio D4 A4 D5 A5 |
| `quest_complete` | quest turned in | 1.15 s | lute run D4 E4 F4 A4 into a bell chord D5 A5 D6 |
| `dialogue_blip` | each dialogue line or character | 0.06 s | 1 kHz sine blip |
| `ui_open`, `ui_close` | inventory, menus | 0.24 s | two soft sines, C5 to G5 opening, G5 to C5 closing |
| `ladder_step` | each rung | 0.11 s | wooden knock (410 / 980 / 1700 Hz) and a tap |
| `respawn` | knight returns after dying | 1.15 s | six-note harp sweep D4 to D6, shimmer noise, a soft D5+A5 pad |

Each builder in `synth.py` (`sfx_<name>`) returns raw audio; `finish_sfx` adds a 1 ms fade-in, a
5 ms fade-out and the -3 dBFS normalisation. Effects that use reverb use the same reverb as the
music, with a short tail.

## The music

Both themes are built from the same palette, which is what gives the game a consistent sound:

- **Lute / psaltery / bass** (`pluck`): a damped harmonic stack. Partial *k* has amplitude
  |sin(pi k p)| / k^alpha, the comb of a string plucked at position *p* (0.14 for the lute, 0.22
  for the bass, 0.30 for the harp), a slight inharmonicity, and a decay time that shortens with
  *k*, so the note opens bright and mellows as it rings. A 8 ms pick-noise burst, two wooden body
  resonances (215 and 470 Hz) and a gentle damping after the written duration finish it. Harder
  plucks are brighter (alpha falls with velocity).
- **Flute** (`flute`): a sine with a little 2nd and 3rd harmonic that grows with breath pressure,
  a short upward onset glide, vibrato that arrives after 120 ms, and band-passed breath noise that
  is loudest at the attack.
- **Hand drum** (`drum_dum`, `drum_tek`): a pitch-dropping sine (157 to 62 Hz) under a low-passed
  noise slap for the open stroke; a band-passed click with two faint ringing partials for the tap.
- **Drone** (`drone`): detuned pairs of harmonic stacks with slow amplitude shimmer, a bourdon
  that runs for the whole loop.
- **Reverb** (`reverb`): Schroeder/Moorer feedback delay, eight low-pass-damped combs into four
  all-passes, 20 ms pre-delay, mixed at a stated RMS ratio under the dry signal.

Dynamics come from several layers: each phrase has its own base velocity; downbeats, `!`-marked
notes and higher notes play a little louder, `~` softer; every note gets a small random velocity
and timing wobble (deterministic). Tunes are written as strings such as `D4:1 E4:1 F4:1.5 E4:.5`
(note:duration, `r` for a rest, `|` for a bar line), so they are easy to read and edit in
`synth.py`.

### `town_theme.ogg`: D Dorian, 4/4, 92 bpm, 24 bars, 62.6 s

Calm, for the town. Plucked lute melody over a D-A drone, a plucked bass and a light hand drum.

| Bars | Phrase | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1-8 | A | the tune, in the D4-B4 range, two four-bar halves (question ending on A, answer settling on D), with rests after each half; bass on roots and fifths; drum: open stroke on 1, tap on 3 |
| 9-16 | B | contrast: starts on A, climbs through C5-D5 to a peak on E5, uses the Dorian B natural; the flute shadows the lute softly; bass walks more; fuller drum pattern (dum, tek, dum, tek) |
| 17-24 | A' | the tune again with passing eighth notes, a second lute answering long notes a third below (an open fifth under the final D), a tap before the downbeat; ends on a held D |

The tempo is 91.9996 bpm (a quarter note is exactly 28,761 samples) so the loop is a whole number
of samples, 2,761,056.

### `outskirts_theme.ogg`: G Mixolydian, 6/8, 96 bpm, 48 bars, 60.0 s

Brighter and a little more adventurous, for the land outside the walls. Flute lead over a G-D
drone, lute arpeggios in open fifths (G, C, D, F) and the hand drum in a lilting 6/8.

| Bars | Phrase | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1-8 | A | the flute tune alone over the drone with one low lute note per bar: setting out |
| 9-16 | A | the same tune with the lute arpeggio (root, fifth, octave, fifth) and the drum joining |
| 17-24 | B | higher, more restless: starts on D5, leans on the Mixolydian F natural, half-cadence on D |
| 25-32 | B' | the B tune driven up to its peak on G5, the lute shadowing the flute (heterophony); the loudest point |
| 33-40 | A' | the tune with eighth-note ornaments |
| 41-48 | A'' | the tune calmly, softer arpeggio, no drum, so the loop lands back on the bare opening |

An eighth note is exactly 9,188 samples (95.97 bpm for the dotted quarter); the loop is 2,646,144
samples.

### Why the loops are seamless

1. Every note that rings past the end of the loop is folded back onto its start, so the loop is
   one period of an endlessly repeating performance, not a performance that was cut.
2. Every drone frequency and modulation rate is quantised to a whole number of cycles per loop,
   so the drone is exactly periodic.
3. The reverb runs over a 6 s pre-roll of the loop's end before the loop is captured, so the
   captured period is the reverb's steady state. The script then renders 64 samples past the
   loop end and compares them with the loop start: the "seam" column of the report, about 1e-11.
4. The Ogg is encoded with the exact sample count (checked by decoding it), so Godot's loop
   restarts on the right sample. Nothing is faded at either end; the drone and the reverb tail
   carry across the boundary.

Both themes are set to the same loudness (-17.5 dBFS RMS, peaks capped at -1 dBFS) so changing
maps does not change level. The town theme ends on a held note and its final half second is
quieter than its first, which is the music breathing before the repeat, not a fade.

What the columns mean and what fails the run:

- `dur_s`: effects must be 0.05 to 1.2 s, music 48 to 64 s.
- `peak_dB`: effects must sit at -3 dBFS (±0.1); music is capped at -1 dBFS.
- `rms_dB`: loudness; music targets -17.5 dBFS.
- `nan`: any NaN in the render fails.
- `seam`: largest difference between the 64 samples after the loop end (as the reverb would have
  kept producing them) and the 64 samples at the loop start; must be under 1e-5.
- `edge rms`: RMS of the first and last 0.5 s, to prove neither end is faded (fails under -40 dB).
- `ogg samples`: decoded sample count must equal the loop length exactly.
- `ogg seam step`: the jump from the last decoded sample to the first, relative to the music's
  own 99.9th-percentile sample-to-sample step; must stay under 3x.
- The whole folder must stay under 4 MiB.

Two more checks were run by hand on the WAV masters and are easy to repeat: a click detector
(largest sample-to-sample jump against the 99.9th percentile: 3.2x for the town theme, 1.8x for
the outskirts, both at drum and pluck onsets, none mid-note) and log-frequency spectrograms, which
show the phrase structure, the rests and the dynamics described above.

## Changing things

- A new effect: add `sfx_<name>` to `synth.py`, register it in `SFX_BUILDERS`, run the script,
  call `SoundBank.play_sfx(&"<name>")`.
- A new tune: write the melody strings, a bass or arpeggio plan and a drum pattern the way
  `town_theme()` and `outskirts_theme()` do, keep the written-unit length in samples an integer,
  register it in `MUSIC_BUILDERS`.
- Level balance between instruments lives in the `score.mix({...})` call of each theme; between
  music and effects in `SoundBank.music_volume_db` and the per-call `volume_db`.
